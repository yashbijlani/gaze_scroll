This page is the future scroll target. For now it is just a stand-in article so you have something natural to look at while testing gaze tracking, calibration accuracy, and logging.

Eye-tracked scrolling will come later, built on top of the signal pipeline being validated here: raw predictions from WebGazer, a One Euro smoothing filter, a dispersion-based fixation detector, and a confidence proxy for every sample.

Try looking at the four corners and edges of the screen. Watch the gaze cursor and the log panel. Corners are usually the hardest region for webcam regression models — note where the estimate breaks down.

Good lighting on your face, no strong backlight, and a stable head position give the best results. Glasses with reflections and low light degrade the track noticeably.

Calibration records labelled samples (you look at a dot, then click it) and feeds them to WebGazer's ridge regression. Everyday clicks and cursor moves keep training it implicitly while you browse.

Webcam eye tracking

This prototype uses your webcam to estimate where you are looking. All video processing happens on this device — no video frames are sent anywhere.

Requires a secure context (localhost or HTTPS) and a Chromium/Firefox/Safari browser.
