Reading sample

This page is the future scroll target. For now it is just a stand-in article so you have something natural to look at while testing gaze tracking, calibration accuracy, and logging.

Eye-tracked scrolling will come later, built on top of the signal pipeline being validated here: raw predictions from WebGazer, a One Euro smoothing filter, a dispersion-based fixation detector, and a confidence proxy for every sample.

Try looking at the four corners and edges of the screen. Watch the gaze cursor and the log panel. Corners are usually the hardest region for webcam regression models — note where the estimate breaks down.

Good lighting on your face, no strong backlight, and a stable head position give the best results. Glasses with reflections and low light degrade the track noticeably.

Calibration records labelled samples (you look at a dot, then click it) and feeds them to WebGazer's ridge regression. Everyday clicks and cursor moves keep training it implicitly while you browse.

Scroll mode is off by default. When you enable automatic scrolling below, the system watches for sustained, confident downward or upward gaze — brief glances never scroll. Your mouse wheel, keys, or touch always override it instantly, and the red Stop button kills it immediately.

Section two — giving the page room to scroll

This extra content exists so there is actually somewhere to go. Read naturally from top to bottom. When your gaze works down through the text and approaches the lower part of the window with steady movement, a reading-aware mode should gently reveal the next lines and stop the moment you settle back into reading.

If scrolling ever feels wrong — too eager, too sluggish, or just surprising — that is data. Open the Gaze Lab, record a session, and replay it against a different mode. The point of this build is to compare strategies, not to declare one winner.

Section three — scanning versus reading

Try skimming quickly across headings versus settling on a paragraph. Fast, broad movement should read as scanning and hold the page still. Slow, stepwise downward fixations through body text are the signature the reading mode looks for.

Edges are informative but weak evidence on their own. Looking at the bottom chrome to check the time must not scroll the page; only sustained, confident, downward-persisting gaze earns motion. When in doubt the engine answers uncertain and does nothing.

Section four — the last stretch

You are nearly at the end of the sample. Gaze-driven scrolling must respect document limits: pressing against the bottom of the page with nowhere left to go should never claim intent. The engine reports reading or idle there instead.

That is the whole tour. Recalibrate if accuracy drifts, export a session when something interesting happens, and note the setup (lighting, glasses, posture) alongside it.

Privacy

Webcam access is required for gaze estimation. All video processing happens on this device — no video frames are sent anywhere, recorded, or persisted. Session exports contain only anonymized gaze-derived numbers (coordinates, confidence, intent), never images, and only when you explicitly click export. Calibration metadata stays in this browser's local storage. Stopping tracking releases the camera immediately.
